# Junctional Rhythms

**Junctional rhythms** originate in the AV node or the bundle of His — the junction between the atria and ventricles. Because the impulse originates below the atria, it depolarizes the ventricles normally (narrow QRS) but activates the atria in reverse (retrograde P waves).

## Types

| Type | Rate | Clinical context |
|---|---|---|
| **Junctional escape rhythm** | 40–60 bpm | Normal backup when sinus fails |
| **Accelerated junctional rhythm** | 60–100 bpm | Enhanced automaticity (dig toxicity, inferior MI) |
| **Junctional tachycardia** | > 100 bpm | Rare; can occur with digitalis toxicity or post-cardiac surgery |

## EKG features

- **Narrow QRS** (unless aberrant conduction or BBB)
- **Retrograde P waves**: inverted in II, III, aVF (because atria depolarize from bottom to top)
- **P wave relationship to QRS**:
  - P before QRS with short PR (< 120 ms): impulse reaches atria before ventricles
  - P buried in QRS: simultaneous atrial and ventricular activation
  - P after QRS (RP < PR): ventricles depolarize first, atria second
- **Regular rhythm**

## Junctional escape — the safety net

When the sinus node fails (extreme bradycardia, complete heart block, sinus arrest), the AV node fires at its intrinsic rate of 40–60 bpm as a **backup pacemaker**. This is a protective mechanism.

If you see a junctional escape rhythm, the sinus node or AV conduction has failed — find out why.

## Accelerated junctional rhythm

Rate 60–100 bpm — faster than normal for the AV node. This occurs when AV nodal automaticity is enhanced. Classic causes:
- **Digitalis toxicity** (very classic)
- **Inferior MI** (increased vagal tone + ischemia)
- Post-cardiac surgery

## Key differential

- **Junctional** vs **sinus with short PR**: In sinus, the P wave is upright in II. In junctional, it is inverted. Check P wave axis.
- **Junctional** vs **ventricular**: In junctional, the QRS is narrow. Wide QRS with no preceding P → ventricular origin.
