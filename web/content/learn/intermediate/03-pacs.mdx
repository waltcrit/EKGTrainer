# Premature Atrial Contractions (PACs)

A **premature atrial contraction (PAC)** — also called an atrial premature beat (APB) or supraventricular premature beat — is an ectopic atrial impulse that fires before the next expected sinus beat.

## EKG features

- **Premature P wave** — occurs earlier than expected; morphology differs from sinus P (different origin)
- **Abnormal P wave axis** — may be inverted, biphasic, or hidden in the preceding T wave
- **PR interval** — may be shorter or longer than normal (depends on distance from AV node)
- **QRS complex** — usually narrow and normal (conducted normally through the ventricles)
- **Non-compensatory pause** — after the PAC, the sinus node resets; the pause is less than twice the normal R–R interval
- **QRS may be absent** — if the PAC arrives when the AV node is still refractory (blocked PAC)
- **Wide QRS** — if the PAC arrives when one bundle branch is still refractory (aberrantly conducted PAC — looks like BBB)

## Non-compensatory vs compensatory pause

After a PAC: **non-compensatory** (sinus node resets)
After a PVC: **compensatory** (sinus node NOT reset; the pause equals exactly two R–R intervals)

This is a useful but not foolproof distinction.

## Clinical significance

PACs are extremely common — found in almost all normal adults with ambulatory monitoring. They are benign in isolation.

**When PACs matter:**
- Very frequent PACs (> 10,000/day) → can cause palpitations, and in some cases trigger atrial fibrillation
- PACs in the setting of structural heart disease → higher arrhythmia burden
- Blocked PACs → can mimic Mobitz II or sinus exit block if not recognized
