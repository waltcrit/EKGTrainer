# Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)

**Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)** is an umbrella term for any fast rhythm originating above the bundle of His. In clinical shorthand, "SVT" most often refers to **AVNRT** (AV nodal reentrant tachycardia) — the most common paroxysmal SVT.

## General EKG features of SVT

- **Rate**: 150–250 bpm (usually 160–200 bpm)
- **Rhythm**: Regular (or almost regular)
- **QRS**: Narrow (≤ 120 ms) — unless aberrant conduction
- **Onset/offset**: Abrupt ("paroxysmal") — sudden start and stop
- **P waves**: Often invisible (buried in QRS or T wave) or retrograde (inverted in II, visible just after QRS)

## Most common SVT types

### AVNRT (AV Nodal Reentrant Tachycardia)
- Most common; reentry circuit within the AV node
- P waves buried in or just after QRS (pseudo-S in II, pseudo-R in V1)
- Short RP interval (RP < PR)

### AVRT (AV Reentrant Tachycardia)
- Requires an accessory pathway (e.g., WPW)
- P waves visible after QRS (slightly longer RP interval)
- Orthodromic AVRT: narrow QRS (normal direction)
- Antidromic AVRT: wide QRS (uses accessory pathway antegrade)

### Atrial tachycardia
- Ectopic atrial focus firing rapidly
- P waves present but different morphology from sinus
- Isoelectric baseline between P waves (unlike flutter)

## SVT vs sinus tachycardia

At rates of 150 bpm, these can be difficult to distinguish. Key differences:
- **Sinus tachy**: gradual onset; rate slows with vagal maneuvers (temporarily); visible P waves with normal morphology
- **SVT**: abrupt onset; vagal maneuvers may terminate it suddenly; P waves hidden or retrograde

## Treatment

- **Vagal maneuvers** (Valsalva, carotid sinus massage, ice water facial immersion) — first line
- **Adenosine** 6 mg rapid IV push (12 mg if no response) — terminates AVNRT/AVRT by blocking AV node; diagnostic if it reveals flutter or atrial tachycardia
- **Rate control**: diltiazem, metoprolol — if adenosine fails or recurs
- **Synchronized cardioversion** — if hemodynamically unstable
