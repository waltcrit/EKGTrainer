# Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)

A **premature ventricular contraction (PVC)** is an ectopic beat originating in the ventricular myocardium, below the bundle of His. Because it doesn't use the normal conduction system, it produces a wide, bizarre QRS.

## EKG features

- **Wide QRS**: > 120 ms (usually > 140 ms)
- **Bizarre morphology**: different from the sinus QRS; notched, slurred
- **No preceding P wave** (or P wave occurs independently — the sinus P "marches through")
- **T wave discordant**: opposite direction to main QRS deflection
- **Full compensatory pause**: sinus P fires on time but finds the ventricle refractory; the R–R interval surrounding the PVC equals exactly 2× the normal R–R interval
- **Interpolated PVC** (no pause): PVC sandwiched between two normal beats with no pause — occurs at slower heart rates

## Uniform vs multiform PVCs

- **Uniform (unifocal)**: All PVCs have the same morphology → same ectopic focus
- **Multiform (multifocal)**: PVCs with different morphologies → multiple foci, higher burden, more concerning

## Patterns

| Pattern | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Bigeminy** | PVC every other beat |
| **Trigeminy** | PVC every third beat |
| **Couplet** | Two consecutive PVCs |
| **Triplet** | Three consecutive PVCs (= run of VT) |
| **R-on-T** | PVC falls on previous T wave → high risk of triggering VT/VF |

## Clinical significance

Isolated, rare PVCs in a structurally normal heart are benign. PVCs are common — found in 40–75% of normal people on Holter monitors.

**When PVCs matter:**
- Frequent PVCs (> 10,000/day or > 10–15% of total beats) → PVC-induced cardiomyopathy
- PVCs in ischemia / recent MI → higher risk of VT/VF
- R-on-T phenomenon → can trigger VF
- Symptomatic (palpitations, reduced ejection fraction) → consider ablation
