# Idioventricular Rhythm (IVR)

**Idioventricular rhythm** is a ventricular escape rhythm originating in the ventricular myocardium or Purkinje fibers, with an intrinsic rate of **20–40 bpm** (some sources say up to 40–50 bpm).

It is the heart's **last-resort backup pacemaker** — activated only when all higher pacemakers (sinus node, AV node) fail.

## EKG features

- **Rate**: 20–40 bpm (very slow)
- **Wide, bizarre QRS**: > 120 ms (often > 160 ms), completely different morphology from sinus beats
- **No P waves** preceding QRS (or P waves may be present but completely dissociated)
- **Regular** (slightly)
- **T wave discordant**: opposite to main QRS deflection

## Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm (AIVR)

Rate **40–100 bpm** — faster than typical IVR but still originating in the ventricle. The term "accelerated" means enhanced ventricular automaticity, not tachycardia.

AIVR is common after reperfusion (post-thrombolysis or primary PCI for STEMI) — it is often a **benign reperfusion rhythm** and does not require treatment in this context.

Other causes: ischemia, cardiomyopathy, digoxin toxicity, cocaine.

## Clinical significance

| Type | Rate | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **IVR** | 20–40 bpm | Life-threatening; support the patient; find the cause |
| **AIVR** | 40–100 bpm | Often benign (reperfusion); monitor |

An idioventricular rhythm at 30 bpm in a pulseless or near-pulseless patient = **PEA or near-PEA** → CPR and epinephrine per ACLS.

## Differentials

- **VT**: rate > 100 bpm; wide QRS; often hemodynamically unstable
- **Junctional escape**: rate 40–60; narrow QRS (normally)
- **3rd-degree AV block**: P waves march through independently at faster rate; escape QRS is wide if ventricular origin
