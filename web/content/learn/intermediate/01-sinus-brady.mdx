# Sinus Bradycardia

**Sinus bradycardia** is sinus rhythm with a rate < 60 bpm. Everything else about the rhythm is normal: regular, P before every QRS, normal PR and QRS.

## Diagnostic criteria

- Rate: **< 60 bpm**
- P waves: upright in II, present before every QRS
- PR: 120–200 ms (normal)
- QRS: ≤ 120 ms (narrow)
- Rhythm: regular

## Common causes

**Physiologic (normal):**
- Athletes at rest (rates of 40–50 bpm can be normal)
- Sleep
- Vagal maneuvers

**Pathologic:**
- Inferior MI (RCA occlusion → SA node ischemia)
- Sick sinus syndrome
- Hypothyroidism
- Hypothermia
- Increased intracranial pressure (Cushing reflex)
- Medications: beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin, amiodarone

## Is it symptomatic?

The key clinical question is not the rate — it's whether the patient is symptomatic:
- Asymptomatic sinus bradycardia in an athlete → no treatment needed
- Symptomatic bradycardia (syncope, near-syncope, hemodynamic compromise) → treat

## Treatment (if symptomatic)

- **Atropine** 0.5–1 mg IV (up to 3 mg total) — first line
- **Dopamine** or **epinephrine** infusion — second line
- **Transcutaneous pacing** — if pharmacologic treatment fails
- Transvenous pacemaker — for refractory or anticipated chronic bradycardia
