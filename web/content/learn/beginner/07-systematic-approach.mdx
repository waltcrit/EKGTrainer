# A Systematic Approach to Reading an EKG

The most important habit you can build as an EKG reader is this: **read every EKG the same way, every time.**

Expert readers don't skip steps because a pattern is "obvious." They follow the same sequence on every strip — for good reason. The dangerous rhythms are often found in the step you would have skipped.

## The 10-Step Method

Use this checklist on every strip you read. Step through it in order, even if a diagnosis seems clear after step 2.

## Why these steps, in this order?

**Step 1 (Basics)** catches technical errors before they mislead you. A reversed lead or a poorly placed electrode looks like pathology.

**Steps 2–3 (Rate + Rhythm)** establish the fundamental behavior of the heart. Everything else is interpreted in this context.

**Steps 4–5 (P waves + PR)** define the relationship between atrium and ventricle — the key to AV block and junctional rhythms.

**Step 6 (QRS)** determines if conduction below the AV node is normal. Wide QRS = big differential.

**Step 7 (ST/T)** is where you find ischemia, infarction, pericarditis, and repolarization abnormalities.

**Step 8 (QT)** is easy to skip — and missing a prolonged QT can cost a life (torsades de pointes).

**Step 9 (Compare)** puts everything in context. A "new" change is always more significant than a "chronic" one.

**Step 10 (Synthesize)** is where you put it all together. State the rhythm, the rate, and the key findings — in that order.

## A word on pattern recognition

As you read more EKGs, you will start to recognize rhythms at a glance. This is good. But don't let pattern recognition replace the systematic approach — use it to generate a hypothesis at step 1, then confirm or deny it with each subsequent step.

The systematic approach is what catches the thing you weren't expecting.
