# The QRS Complex

The **QRS complex** represents **ventricular depolarization** — the electrical wave spreading through the ventricles, causing them to contract and eject blood.

It is the most prominent feature on any rhythm strip: tall, sharp, and narrow (in normal conduction).

---

## Electrical meaning

The QRS is the ventricles switching on electrically.

- Normal path: AV node -> His bundle -> right/left bundle branches -> Purkinje network.
- Fast conduction through this pathway creates a **narrow** QRS.
- If conduction is delayed or bypassed, QRS becomes **wide**.

---

## Anatomy of the QRS

| Component | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Q wave** | Initial small negative deflection (septal depolarization) |
| **R wave** | Positive deflection (main ventricular depolarization) |
| **S wave** | Terminal negative deflection after the R |

Not all QRS complexes have all three components. The dominant deflection determines naming:
- All negative → **QS complex**
- No Q → **RS complex**
- No S → **QR complex**

---

## Normal QRS duration

Normal: **≤ 120 ms** (≤ 3 small boxes)

---

## Beginner recognition checklist

- Find the tallest sharp spike on the strip (usually R wave) and define QRS start and end.
- Measure width in small boxes: normal is **≤ 3 small boxes**.
- Narrow QRS suggests supraventricular origin with normal ventricular conduction.
- Wide QRS suggests delayed ventricular activation (bundle branch block, ventricular rhythm, paced beat, or metabolic/toxic cause).
- Pathological Q-wave clue: width **≥ 1 small box (40 ms)** or depth **≥ 25% of the following R wave** in contiguous leads.

### Wide-QRS differential

A wide QRS (> 120 ms) indicates delayed ventricular conduction — think:
- Bundle branch block (RBBB or LBBB)
- Ventricular rhythm (VT, accelerated idioventricular)
- Aberrant conduction
- Pacemaker-paced beat
- Hyperkalemia (diffuse QRS widening)
- Pre-excitation (WPW) — short PR + delta wave

---

## QRS amplitude

- Low voltage: all limb leads < 5 mm or all precordial leads < 10 mm → think pericardial effusion, hypothyroidism, obesity, COPD
- Tall R waves in lateral leads with deep S in V1 → LVH pattern

---

## Axis

The **QRS axis** describes the net direction of ventricular depolarization.

| Axis | Lead I | aVF | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Positive | Positive | 0° to +90° |
| LAD | Positive | Negative | −30° to −90° |
| RAD | Negative | Positive | +90° to +180° |

Quick rule: Lead I (+) and aVF (+) → normal axis.

---

## Key teaching point

The QRS complex is the anchor of every EKG read. Before looking at P waves or intervals, establish the QRS rate, regularity, and width. Everything else is interpreted relative to the QRS.
