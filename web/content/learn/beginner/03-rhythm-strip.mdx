# Reading a Rhythm Strip

A **rhythm strip** is a continuous recording of a single lead — most commonly lead II or lead V1 — printed at 25 mm/s.

It is the most common way to assess **rate and rhythm**, and it's what you'll practice most in this trainer.

## The ECG paper grid

ECG paper has two grid sizes:

| Box | Size | Time | Voltage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small box | 1 × 1 mm | 40 ms | 0.1 mV |
| Large box | 5 × 5 mm | 200 ms | 0.5 mV |

This is critical for measurement. Five large boxes = 1 second.

## What to look for on a rhythm strip

When you pick up a rhythm strip, scan it from left to right and ask:

1. **Are there QRS complexes?** (Tall, narrow spikes — the most obvious feature)
2. **Are they regular?** (Space between them consistent?)
3. **Are there P waves?** (Small bumps before each QRS?)
4. **Does each P have a QRS, and each QRS have a P?**

These four questions alone will get you most of the way to a diagnosis.

## Calipers — your best tool

ECG calipers let you march out intervals without eyeballing. Set them to one R–R interval, then step across the strip. If every step lands exactly on an R wave, the rhythm is regular.

In this trainer, you can use the **Measure Interval** tool to practice dragging across intervals.

## Lead II vs V1

- **Lead II** is best for seeing P waves (upright and clear when sinus)
- **V1** is best for distinguishing right vs left bundle branch blocks, and for seeing flutter waves

Most rhythm interpretations use lead II as the primary strip.
