# Calculating Heart Rate

Rate is one of the first things you assess in any systematic EKG read. Here are three methods, from fastest to most precise.

## Method 1: The 300 rule (regular rhythms only)

Count the number of **large boxes** between two consecutive R waves. Divide 300 by that number.

| Large boxes | Rate (bpm) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 300 |
| 2 | 150 |
| 3 | 100 |
| 4 | 75 |
| 5 | 60 |
| 6 | 50 |

**Mnemonic:** 300 → 150 → 100 → 75 → 60 → 50

This only works if the rhythm is **regular**. Use method 2 or 3 for irregular rhythms.

## Method 2: The 6-second strip method (irregular rhythms)

Count the number of QRS complexes in a 6-second strip (30 large boxes) and multiply by 10.

Or count in a 10-second strip and multiply by 6.

Most rhythm strips are printed at 10 seconds — look for the tick marks or hash marks at the top.

## Method 3: Caliper/formula (most precise)

Measure the R–R interval in milliseconds, then:

**Rate = 60,000 ÷ R–R interval (ms)**

For example, R–R = 800 ms → 60,000 / 800 = **75 bpm**

## Rate categories

| Category | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Bradycardia | < 60 bpm |
| Normal | 60–100 bpm |
| Tachycardia | > 100 bpm |

For atrial flutter with 2:1 block: atrial rate ~300 bpm, ventricular rate ~150 bpm.

## Don't forget the atrial rate

In rhythms where the atrial and ventricular rates differ (e.g., AV block, flutter), calculate both:
- **Atrial rate**: count P-to-P intervals
- **Ventricular rate**: count R-to-R intervals

Report both: "Atrial rate 300, ventricular rate 75 — consistent with atrial flutter with 4:1 block."
