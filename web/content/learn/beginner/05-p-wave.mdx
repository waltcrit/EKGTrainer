# The P Wave

The **P wave** represents **atrial depolarization** — the electrical signal spreading through both atria, causing them to contract and push blood into the ventricles.

## Normal P wave characteristics

| Feature | Normal value |
|---|---|
| Duration | < 120 ms (3 small boxes) |
| Amplitude | < 2.5 mm (2.5 small boxes) in lead II |
| Axis | Upright in I, II; inverted in aVR |
| Morphology | Smooth, rounded |

## Why the P wave matters

If there's a P wave before every QRS and it's upright in lead II → the impulse started in the **sinus node**. This is the definition of sinus rhythm.

If P waves are:
- **Absent** → Atrial fibrillation, flutter, or junctional/ventricular rhythm
- **Inverted in II** → Retrograde conduction (junctional, or posterior atrial focus)
- **Sawtooth pattern** → Atrial flutter
- **More P waves than QRS** → AV block

## P wave morphology clues

| Finding | Think |
|---|---|
| Broad, notched P (P mitrale) | Left atrial enlargement |
| Tall, peaked P (P pulmonale) | Right atrial enlargement |
| Biphasic P in V1 | Combined or left atrial abnormality |
| Variable P morphology | Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT) |
