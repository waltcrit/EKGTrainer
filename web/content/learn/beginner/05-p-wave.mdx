# The P Wave

The **P wave** represents **atrial depolarization** — the electrical signal spreading through both atria, causing them to contract and push blood into the ventricles.

<DiagramPQRST highlight="p" />

---

## Electrical meaning

The P wave is the atria turning on electrically.

- It starts in the **SA node** and spreads across the right and left atria.
- Mechanical result: atrial contraction (the "atrial kick") that tops off ventricular filling.
- If P waves are normal and consistently before QRS, the rhythm origin is usually sinus.

---

## Normal P wave characteristics

| Feature | Normal value |
| --- | --- |
| Duration | < 120 ms (3 small boxes) |
| Amplitude | < 2.5 mm (2.5 small boxes) in lead II |
| Axis | Upright in I, II; inverted in aVR |
| Morphology | Smooth, rounded |

---

## Beginner recognition checklist

- Look just before each QRS for a small rounded deflection.
- Count width in small boxes: normal is **< 3 small boxes**.
- Check height in lead II: normal is **< 2.5 small boxes**.
- Check direction: usually upright in I and II, inverted in aVR.
- Sinus clue: one similar-looking P wave before each QRS.

---

## Why the P wave matters

If there's a P wave before every QRS and it's upright in lead II, the impulse started in the **sinus node**. This is sinus rhythm.

### Interpretation shortcuts

| Finding | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| **Absent P waves** | Atrial fibrillation, flutter, or junctional/ventricular rhythm |
| **Inverted P waves in lead II** | Retrograde conduction (junctional rhythm or low atrial focus) |
| **Sawtooth atrial activity** | Atrial flutter |
| **More P waves than QRS complexes** | AV block |

---

## P wave morphology clues

| Finding | Think |
| --- | --- |
| Broad, notched P (P mitrale) | Left atrial enlargement |
| Tall, peaked P (P pulmonale) | Right atrial enlargement |
| Biphasic P in V1 | Combined or left atrial abnormality |
| Variable P morphology | Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT) |
