# The 12-Lead ECG

A 12-lead ECG gives you 12 different "views" of the heart's electrical activity, recorded simultaneously from electrodes placed on the limbs and chest.

## Standard layout

Most 12-lead ECGs are printed in a 3-column × 4-row format:

```
Column 1    Column 2    Column 3
I           aVR         V1   V4
II          aVL         V2   V5
III         aVF         V3   V6
```

At the bottom, a **rhythm strip** (usually lead II) runs the full width.

## Frontal plane leads (limb + augmented)

| Lead | Positive electrode | View |
|---|---|---|
| I | Left arm | Lateral |
| II | Left foot | Inferior |
| III | Left foot | Inferior |
| aVR | Right arm | Rightward |
| aVL | Left arm | Lateral |
| aVF | Left foot | Inferior |

## Precordial leads (chest leads)

| Lead | Position | View |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Right of sternum, 4th ICS | Septal |
| V2 | Left of sternum, 4th ICS | Septal |
| V3 | Between V2 and V4 | Anterior |
| V4 | Midclavicular line, 5th ICS | Anterior |
| V5 | Anterior axillary line | Lateral |
| V6 | Midaxillary line | Lateral |

## Why it matters

Different regions of the heart are supplied by different coronary arteries. Ischemic changes appear in **contiguous leads** (leads that view the same territory):

- **Inferior**: II, III, aVF → right coronary artery (RCA)
- **Anterior**: V1–V4 → left anterior descending (LAD)
- **Lateral**: I, aVL, V5–V6 → left circumflex (LCx)

This becomes essential in the Advanced module on STEMI localization.

## For now

As a beginner, focus on the **rhythm strip** (lead II). As you advance, you'll use the full 12-lead to assess:
- Axis (lead I + aVF)
- Ischemia (ST changes in contiguous leads)
- Bundle branch blocks (morphology in V1 and V6)
