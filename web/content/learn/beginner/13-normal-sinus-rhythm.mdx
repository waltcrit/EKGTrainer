# Normal Sinus Rhythm

**Normal sinus rhythm (NSR)** is the standard, healthy baseline. Every other rhythm is defined by how it deviates from NSR.

## Diagnostic criteria

For a rhythm to be called NSR, **all five** of the following must be true:

1. **Rate**: 60–100 bpm
2. **Rhythm**: Regular (R–R intervals consistent, ±10%)
3. **P waves**: Present before every QRS, upright in lead II, same morphology
4. **PR interval**: 120–200 ms (3–5 small boxes), constant
5. **QRS duration**: ≤ 120 ms (≤ 3 small boxes)

If any criterion is violated, the rhythm is not NSR — even if everything else looks normal.

## What NSR looks like

On a lead II rhythm strip:
- Evenly spaced, narrow QRS complexes at 60–100 bpm
- A small, upright P wave before each QRS, separated by a constant PR interval
- After the QRS, a T wave (upright in lead II)
