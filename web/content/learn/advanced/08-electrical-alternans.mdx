# Electrical Alternans

**Electrical alternans** is the beat-to-beat alternation in QRS amplitude (and sometimes axis) on the EKG. It is a classic sign of **cardiac tamponade** — though it can occur in other conditions.

## EKG features

- **Alternating QRS height** (and sometimes morphology) in a regular, beat-to-beat pattern
- The heart physically swings back and forth within the pericardial effusion with each beat, changing its electrical axis from the perspective of each lead
- Most dramatic in the precordial leads (V1–V6)
- May also affect P waves and T waves (total electrical alternans)
- Rhythm is usually sinus tachycardia (tachycardia + alternans + clinical tamponade = Beck's triad precursor)

## Cardiac tamponade physiology

Fluid in the pericardial space compresses the heart. The heart swings pendulum-like within the effusion:
- Beat 1: heart swings one way → one QRS amplitude
- Beat 2: heart swings back → different QRS amplitude
- Result: alternating pattern on EKG

## Clinical context: Beck's Triad

Classic tamponade triad (seen in only ~33% of cases):
1. **Hypotension**
2. **Elevated JVP** (distended neck veins)
3. **Muffled heart sounds**

Other findings: pulsus paradoxus (> 10 mmHg drop in systolic BP during inspiration), Kussmaul's sign absent (unlike constrictive pericarditis).

## Electrical alternans in other conditions

- **Large pericardial effusion** without tamponade (less dramatic alternans)
- **VT** — can cause alternans during the arrhythmia itself
- **Severe LV dysfunction** — beat-to-beat mechanical alternans

## Diagnosis

Confirmed by **echocardiography** — the swinging heart and effusion are directly visualized. EKG alternans in the right clinical context is sufficient to prompt urgent echo.

## Treatment

**Pericardiocentesis** is the definitive treatment for tamponade. This is a medical emergency. IV fluids may temporarily stabilize hemodynamics (maximizes preload) while preparing for drainage.

Do NOT give diuretics or nitrates (reduce preload → cardiovascular collapse).
