# NSTEMI & Ischemia Patterns

Not all acute coronary syndrome (ACS) produces ST elevation. **NSTEMI** and **unstable angina (UA)** present with ischemic EKG changes without ST elevation meeting STEMI criteria.

<DiagramPQRST highlight="st" />

## EKG patterns of subendocardial ischemia

| Pattern | Leads | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Horizontal or downsloping ST depression** ≥ 0.5 mm | Any | Subendocardial ischemia; highly specific if ≥ 1 mm |
| **T-wave inversions** ≥ 1 mm | Symmetric, deep | Ischemia (Wellens, NSTEMI) |
| **Non-specific ST-T changes** | Any | Less specific; consider other causes |
| **Pseudonormalization** of previously inverted T waves | Same territory | Active ischemia in that territory |

## Distinguishing NSTEMI from stable angina on EKG

In stable angina, EKG changes appear during chest pain and resolve at rest. In NSTEMI, changes may persist. This distinction requires serial EKGs and troponin.

## Wellens Syndrome

A warning pattern for critical proximal LAD stenosis after angina has resolved:

| Type | T wave morphology | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wellens A** | Biphasic T in V2–V3 | High — do NOT stress test |
| **Wellens B** | Deep symmetric inversion in V2–V3 | High — do NOT stress test |

Troponin may be normal or mildly elevated. The artery is critically stenosed but not yet occluded. These patients are at imminent risk of anterior STEMI.

## De Winter T-waves

Upsloping ST depression ≥ 1 mm at the J-point with tall, peaked, symmetric T waves in V1–V6. Represents acute LAD occlusion (STEMI equivalent) without ST elevation. This pattern does not evolve to classic STEMI — activate the cath lab immediately.

## Pathological Q waves (old MI pattern)

Pathological Q waves usually reflect prior transmural infarction and myocardial scar rather than active ischemia.

Use practical criteria:

- Q-wave width ≥ 40 ms (≥ 1 small box)
- Q-wave depth ≥ 25% of the following R-wave amplitude
- Presence in contiguous territory leads (for example II, III, aVF for inferior wall)

Isolated small septal q waves can be normal in lateral leads. The key distinction from acute STEMI is that old-MI Q-wave patterns typically lack new contiguous ST elevation.

## Differentiating ischemic from non-ischemic ST depression

| Cause | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Subendocardial ischemia | Horizontal or downsloping; present in related leads |
| Digitalis | "Scooped" (upsloping) ST depression; diffuse |
| LVH strain | Downsloping ST-T in lateral leads (I, aVL, V5–V6) |
| RBBB/LBBB (secondary) | ST-T discordant with QRS; expected pattern |
| Hypokalemia | Flattened T, prominent U wave, ST depression |
| Tachycardia | Rate-related ST depression (false positive) |
