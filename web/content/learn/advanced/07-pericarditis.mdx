# Pericarditis

**Pericarditis** is inflammation of the pericardium. It produces a distinctive EKG pattern caused by diffuse subepicardial injury from the surrounding inflammation — not focal coronary occlusion.

## Classic EKG features

Four stages of pericarditis EKG evolution:

| Stage | Timing | EKG findings |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Days 1–2 | **Diffuse concave (saddle-shaped) STE** + PR depression |
| **II** | Days 3–7 | ST normalizes; T waves flatten |
| **III** | Weeks 1–3 | **Diffuse T-wave inversions** |
| **IV** | Weeks to months | T waves normalize |

Most EKGs show Stage I or a mix of Stage I and II.

## Stage I: the classic pattern

- **Diffuse ST elevation**: Concave ("saddle-shaped") STE in almost all leads **except aVR and V1**
- **PR depression**: Key finding — PR segment is depressed in the same leads showing STE (due to atrial myocarditis/pericarditis)
- **PR elevation in aVR**: Reciprocal finding
- **No reciprocal ST depression** (unlike STEMI, which has focal STE + reciprocal depression)

## Distinguishing pericarditis from STEMI

| Feature | Pericarditis | STEMI |
|---|---|---|
| STE distribution | Diffuse (multiple leads) | Focal (contiguous leads) |
| STE shape | Concave (saddle-shaped) | Convex (tombstone) or flat |
| PR depression | Present | Absent |
| Reciprocal STD | Absent | Present |
| aVR | PR elevation, STE | ST depression |
| Q waves | Absent | Can develop |

## Early repolarization vs pericarditis

Both show concave STE with upright T waves. Clues for pericarditis:
- PR depression (pathognomonic of pericarditis, not seen in ER)
- Diffuse STE (ER tends to be more localized)
- Clinical context: fever, pleuritic chest pain, friction rub

**Spodick's sign**: Downsloping TP segment in multiple leads → favors pericarditis.

## Treatment

- NSAIDs + colchicine (first-line, reduces recurrence)
- Restrict activity until asymptomatic and CRP normalizes
- Avoid corticosteroids initially (increases recurrence risk)
