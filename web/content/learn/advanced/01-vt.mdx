# Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)

**Ventricular tachycardia** is a life-threatening arrhythmia defined as **≥ 3 consecutive ventricular beats** at a rate **> 100 bpm** (usually 100–250 bpm), originating in the ventricular myocardium below the bundle of His.

## EKG features

- **Rate**: 100–250 bpm (monomorphic VT typically 140–200)
- **Wide QRS**: > 120 ms (usually > 140 ms), bizarre morphology
- **Regular rhythm** (monomorphic VT)
- **AV dissociation**: P waves march through independently (if visible)
- **Fusion beats**: Sinus beat + VT beat fusing (proves ventricular origin)
- **Capture beats**: Occasional narrow QRS conducted through from sinus node (proves AV dissociation)

## Monomorphic vs Polymorphic VT

| Type | QRS morphology | Classic causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monomorphic** | All QRS identical | Scar-related (post-MI, cardiomyopathy) |
| **Polymorphic** | QRS morphology changes beat-to-beat | Ischemia, long QT (torsades), catecholaminergic |

## The VT vs SVT with aberrancy problem

This is the classic wide complex tachycardia differential. Default rule: **treat all wide complex tachycardia as VT until proven otherwise.**

**Features favoring VT:**
- AV dissociation (P waves independent of QRS)
- Fusion/capture beats
- QRS > 160 ms (very wide)
- Concordance (all precordial leads positive or all negative)
- Northwest axis (−90° to ±180°, "no man's land")
- RS onset to nadir > 100 ms in any precordial lead

**Brugada algorithm (SVT with aberrancy) — step through in order:**
1. Absence of RS complex in any precordial lead → VT
2. RS interval > 100 ms in any precordial lead → VT
3. AV dissociation → VT
4. Morphology criteria (BBB-like) → if negative → SVT

## Torsades de Pointes

Polymorphic VT associated with a **prolonged QTc**. The QRS complexes twist around the baseline ("twisting of the points"). Can degenerate to VF.

Treatment: magnesium sulfate 2g IV; correct the cause (remove offending drug, treat electrolyte abnormality).

## Treatment of VT

**Pulseless VT**: Defibrillation + CPR per ACLS
**Hemodynamically unstable VT (with pulse)**: Synchronized cardioversion
**Stable monomorphic VT**: Amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min; consider cardioversion if no response
