# Long QT Syndrome

**Long QT syndrome (LQTS)** is a disorder of ventricular repolarization that prolongs the QT interval and predisposes to a specific polymorphic ventricular tachycardia called **Torsades de Pointes (TdP)**, which can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation and sudden death.

## Measuring the QT interval

The QT interval is measured from the start of the QRS complex to the end of the T wave in the lead where it is longest (usually V2 or V3). Because the QT interval varies with heart rate, we use the **corrected QT (QTc)**:

$$QTc = \frac{QT}{\sqrt{RR}}$$

(Bazett's formula — RR in seconds)

**Normal:** QTc <440 ms (men), <460 ms (women)  
**Borderline:** 440–500 ms  
**High risk:** QTc **>500 ms** — significantly elevated risk of TdP

## Causes

### Congenital LQTS
Genetic mutations affecting cardiac ion channels:
- **LQTS1** (KCNQ1 — IKs channel): triggered by exercise/swimming; beta-blockers effective
- **LQTS2** (KCNH2 — IKr channel): triggered by auditory stimuli or sudden arousal; avoid QT-prolonging drugs
- **LQTS3** (SCN5A — INa channel): events during sleep/bradycardia; highest risk of sudden death

### Acquired LQTS (most common)
- **Electrolytes:** hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia
- **Drugs:** antiarrhythmics (sotalol, amiodarone, quinidine), antipsychotics (haloperidol, quetiapine), antibiotics (azithromycin, fluoroquinolones), antihistamines
- **Bradycardia** — any slow rhythm prolongs QT
- **Myocardial ischemia**, hypothyroidism, intracranial hemorrhage

> Always check CredibleMeds (crediblemeds.org) for drug-induced QT risk.

## EKG features

- Prolonged QTc as above
- T wave may appear **broad, notched, or biphasic** (particularly in LQTS2)
- U wave may be prominent (can be confused with the T wave end)
- QRS is normal width (the problem is repolarization, not depolarization)
- No structural heart disease is visible

## Torsades de Pointes

TdP is a polymorphic VT characterized by:
- **"Twisting of the points"** — QRS axis rotates around the isoelectric baseline
- Rate 150–300 bpm, irregular
- Often self-terminating → syncope, but can degenerate to VF

Classic trigger: **short-long-short** RR sequence preceding the first TdP beat (a PVC followed by a compensatory pause then another PVC that initiates TdP).

## Treatment

### Acute TdP
1. **IV Magnesium** (2 g IV bolus) — first-line even if Mg is normal; shortens QT and suppresses EADs
2. **Correct electrolytes** — target K >4.5 mEq/L, Mg >2 mEq/L
3. **Remove offending drugs immediately**
4. **Overdrive pacing** or isoproterenol (to increase rate and shorten QT) if TdP is recurrent and bradycardia-dependent
5. **Defibrillation** if VF

### Long-term / Congenital LQTS
- **Beta-blockers** (especially LQTS1 and LQTS2)
- **ICD** for survivors of cardiac arrest, high-risk congenital forms, or refractory TdP
- Restriction from competitive sports (case-by-case)
- Screen first-degree relatives

### Avoid
- QT-prolonging drugs (see CredibleMeds)
- Hypokalemia / hypomagnesemia
- Sudden auditory stimuli (LQTS2)
