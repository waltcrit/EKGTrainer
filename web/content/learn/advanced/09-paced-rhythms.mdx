# Paced Rhythms

Recognizing pacemaker rhythms is essential in any clinical setting. Failure to recognize an undersensing or non-capturing pacemaker can be life-threatening.

## Pacemaker spikes

The defining feature of a paced beat is a **pacemaker spike** — a sharp, vertical artifact at the start of the paced complex. Older pacemakers produce larger spikes; modern bipolar devices produce very small spikes that can be invisible in some leads.

If you suspect pacing but see no spikes, try multiple leads or adjust gain.

## Types of paced rhythms

### Ventricular pacing (VVI/VOO)

- Pacemaker spike followed immediately by a **wide, bizarre QRS** (like a LBBB pattern if RV apex-paced)
- No preceding P wave (purely ventricular)
- T wave discordant with QRS (expected)
- ST elevation in V1–V3 and ST depression in lateral leads (secondary changes — do NOT interpret as ischemia in this context)

### Atrial pacing (AAI)

- Pacemaker spike followed by a **P wave** (with paced morphology)
- QRS is narrow (normal conduction through AV node and His-Purkinje)

### Dual-chamber pacing (DDD)

- Two spikes per cycle: one before P wave (atrial), one before QRS (ventricular)
- Most common in patients with AV block
- The atrial spike may be very small and hard to see

### Biventricular pacing (CRT — Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy)

- Paces both ventricles simultaneously to resynchronize in heart failure
- QRS becomes narrower than typical LBBB (the whole point)

## Normal pacemaker behavior vs malfunction

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Paced spike with wide QRS → pulse | Normal capture |
| Paced spike with no QRS | **Failure to capture** |
| QRS without preceding spike when rate < programmed rate | **Failure to sense** (undersensing) |
| No spikes when rate < programmed rate | **Failure to pace** (lead fracture, battery, sensing issue) |
| Rate faster than programmed | Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia or magnet response |

## STEMI in a paced patient

ST elevation in a paced rhythm must be interpreted using **Sgarbossa criteria** (same as LBBB):
- Concordant STE ≥ 1 mm (+5 points)
- Concordant ST depression ≥ 1 mm in V1–V3 (+3 points)
- Excessively discordant STE ≥ 5 mm (+2 points)
- Score ≥ 3 → high likelihood of AMI
