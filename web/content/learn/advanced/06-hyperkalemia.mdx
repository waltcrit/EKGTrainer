# Hyperkalemia on the EKG

Hyperkalemia is one of the most important electrolyte abnormalities to recognize on an EKG because it is rapidly progressive and imminently life-threatening if untreated.

## Progression of EKG changes with rising K⁺

EKG changes correlate roughly (but not perfectly) with serum potassium levels:

| Serum K⁺ | EKG finding |
| --- | --- |
| 5.5–6.0 mEq/L | **Peaked (tall, narrow, symmetric) T waves** — "tented" T waves |
| 6.0–6.5 mEq/L | **PR prolongation**, flattened/absent P waves |
| 6.5–7.0 mEq/L | **Widening QRS** |
| > 7.0 mEq/L | Sine wave pattern — QRS and T wave merge |
| > 8.0–9.0 mEq/L | **VF or asystole** |

## Peaked T waves — the first sign

Hyperkalemic T waves are:
- **Tall** (amplitude > 6 mm in limb leads or > 10 mm in precordial leads)
- **Narrow** and **symmetric** (unlike normal asymmetric T waves)
- "Tent-like" in shape
- Present in multiple leads (especially precordial)

## The sine wave pattern

At very high potassium levels, the PR prolongs, P waves disappear, and the widened QRS merges with the T wave, creating a sinusoidal pattern. This is a pre-terminal rhythm — treat aggressively.

## Hyperkalemia vs other causes of peaked T waves

| Cause | T wave features |
| --- | --- |
| **Hyperkalemia** | Narrow, symmetric, tall, multiple leads |
| **Hyperacute STEMI** | Broad, tall, asymmetric; associated with STE |
| **Early repolarization** | Tall T, concave STE, notch at J-point; young, athletic |
| **LVH** | Peaked T in V1–V3 with deep S waves |

## Treatment of hyperkalemia with EKG changes

Act immediately — do not wait for lab confirmation if EKG changes are present:

1. **Calcium gluconate** (or calcium chloride) — stabilizes the cardiac membrane immediately; does NOT lower K⁺
2. **Insulin + D50** (glucose) — shifts K⁺ into cells within 15–30 min
3. **Sodium bicarbonate** — if acidotic; shifts K⁺ intracellularly
4. **Albuterol** nebulized — additional K⁺ shift
5. **Kayexalate / patiromer** — removes K⁺ from body (slow onset)
6. **Emergent dialysis** — if above measures fail or patient is dialysis-dependent
7. **Treat the cause**: AKI, Addison's, medications (ACE/ARBs, K-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs)
