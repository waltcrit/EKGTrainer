# Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW)

**WPW** is a pre-excitation syndrome caused by an **accessory pathway** — an abnormal electrical connection between the atria and ventricles that bypasses the AV node. This pathway is called the **Bundle of Kent**.

## How it happens

Normally, the impulse from the sinus node is delayed at the AV node before activating the ventricles — this produces the PR interval. In WPW, the accessory pathway conducts faster than the AV node, so the ventricles begin depolarizing early **(pre-excitation)** before normal conduction arrives.

The result is a **fusion beat**: part of the ventricle is activated via the pathway (producing the delta wave), and the rest via the normal His-Purkinje system.

## Classic EKG triad

1. **Short PR interval (<120 ms)** — conduction bypasses the AV node delay
2. **Delta wave** — slurred, slow upstroke at the beginning of the QRS
3. **Wide QRS (≥120 ms)** — due to abnormal ventricular activation

Secondary ST-T changes are common and discordant to the delta wave — do not interpret as ischemia.

## The real danger: AFib + WPW

The greatest risk in WPW is **atrial fibrillation with rapid accessory pathway conduction**. Because the pathway lacks the rate-limiting properties of the AV node, atrial impulses at 300–600 bpm can conduct directly to the ventricles at very rapid rates → **ventricular fibrillation**.

### Warning signs on EKG during AFib with WPW
- Extremely rapid, irregular rhythm (>250 bpm)
- Wide, bizarre QRS complexes (pre-excited)
- Variable QRS morphology (fusion of pathway and normal conduction)

## Treatment

### Stable SVT in WPW
Vagal maneuvers → adenosine (if narrow-complex, orthodromic) → cardioversion.

### AFib with WPW (or wide-complex WPW tachycardia)
- **Avoid AV nodal blockers** (adenosine, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin) — they block the AV node but not the pathway, accelerating conduction down the accessory pathway → VF
- Use **procainamide** or **electrical cardioversion**
- Urgent electrophysiology referral

### Definitive treatment
**Catheter ablation** of the accessory pathway is curative (>95% success). All symptomatic patients and asymptomatic patients with high-risk pathways should be referred.

## Localization of the pathway

The delta wave polarity across the 12 leads can identify which side of the AV groove the pathway is located. This is useful for pre-ablation planning but is not required for emergency management.
