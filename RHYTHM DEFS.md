# EKGTrainer Internal Reference

Internal reference compiled from AHA/ACC guidance and standard ECG teaching. Use this document as the authoritative internal source for training content, case labeling, and morphological findings.

## Normal Sinus Rhythm

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Sinus  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** A regular rhythm originating from the sinoatrial node with normal atrioventricular conduction.

**Criteria:**  
- Rate 60–100 bpm
- Regular rhythm
- Upright P wave in lead II before each QRS
- PR interval 120–200 ms
- QRS duration <120 ms
- 1:1 P:QRS ratio

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Normal baseline rhythm
- Serves as the reference pattern for sinus rhythms

- Diagnostic criteria
- Common pitfalls
- Differential diagnosis

**Common Misreads:**  
- Sinus bradycardia
- Sinus tachycardia
- Sinus arrhythmia

- Sinus arrhythmia

**Comments for App Content:**  
- Canonical normal rhythm

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Sinus

- Regular rhythm
- Normal P wave morphology

- Narrow QRS (<120 ms)

## Sinus Arrhythmia

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Sinus  
**Teaching Priority:** Medium  
**Definition:** Sinus rhythm with physiologic variation in heart rate, usually related to respiration.

**Criteria:**  
- Regular P waves with sinus morphology
- Variation in PP intervals and RR intervals
- Cyclic variation related to respiration
- Normal PR interval
- Normal QRS duration
- No ectopic beats

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Normal finding, especially in young people and athletes
- No treatment required

## Atrial Fibrillation

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Atrial  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Irregular supraventricular rhythm with disorganized atrial activity and no discrete P waves.

**Criteria:**  
- Irregularly irregular RR intervals
- No discrete P waves
- Fibrillatory baseline may be present
- Variable ventricular response

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Common cause of palpitations and stroke risk
- May be fast or rate controlled
- Distinguish from atrial flutter with variable block and frequent PACs

## Atrial Flutter

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Atrial  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Regular atrial tachyarrhythmia with organized atrial activity and characteristic flutter waves.

**Criteria:**  
- Flutter waves at a regular atrial rate, typically 250–350 bpm
- Sawtooth appearance, often best seen in inferior leads and V1
- Fixed or variable AV block
- Ventricular rhythm may be regular or irregular depending on block ratio
- QRS usually narrow unless aberrancy is present

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Often confused with atrial fibrillation when AV block is variable
- Common block ratios include 2:1, 3:1, and 4:1
- Can be difficult to detect if flutter waves are buried in the QRS or T wave

## Premature Atrial Contractions

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Atrial  
**Teaching Priority:** Medium  
**Definition:** Early atrial depolarizations arising outside the sinoatrial node.

**Criteria:**  
- Premature beat relative to sinus rhythm
- P wave morphology differs from sinus P waves
- Usually followed by a QRS complex
- Pause after the beat is usually non-compensatory
- PR interval may differ from sinus beats
- Can be blocked if the impulse arrives too early

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Common and often benign
- Can trigger SVT or atrial fibrillation if frequent
- Blocked PACs may mimic sinus pauses or AV block

## Supraventricular Tachycardia

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Supraventricular  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Rapid narrow-complex tachycardia originating above the ventricles, most commonly due to AV nodal reentry or AV reentry.

**Criteria:**  
- Heart rate typically 140–250 bpm
- Regular narrow-complex tachycardia
- Abrupt onset and termination
- P waves may be hidden in the QRS or T wave
- Often 1:1 AV conduction
- May be adenosine-responsive

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Commonly due to AVNRT, AVRT, or atrial tachycardia
- Can be mistaken for sinus tachycardia if onset is not captured
- Rate alone is not sufficient to distinguish from sinus tachycardia

**Common Misreads:**

## Junctional Rhythm

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Junctional  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Rhythm originating from the AV junction rather than the sinus node.

**Criteria:**  
- Rate typically 40–60 bpm
- Regular rhythm
- Narrow QRS complexes
- P waves absent, inverted, or retrograde
- P waves may occur before, during, or after the QRS
- AV dissociation may be present

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Often functions as an escape rhythm
- Can occur with sinus node dysfunction or increased vagal tone
- Retrograde P waves may be subtle

## 1st Degree AV Block

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** AV block  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Delayed AV conduction with all atrial impulses conducted to the ventricles.

**Criteria:**  
- PR interval >200 ms
- Every P wave followed by a QRS complex
- Regular rhythm
- QRS duration may be normal or abnormal depending on coexisting disease
- 1:1 AV conduction preserved

**Clinical Notes:**  
- May be a normal variant or related to medication or conduction system disease
- Usually does not require treatment if isolated

**Suggested Learning Sections:**  
- Recognition basics
- Diagnostic criteria
- Clinical significance
- Differential diagnosis

**Common Misreads:**  
- Mobitz I
- Sinus rhythm with long PR from artifact
- Junctional rhythm

**Related Topics:**  
- Mobitz I
- Mobitz II
- AV conduction delay

**Comments for App Content:**  
- Emphasize that conduction is delayed, not dropped

## 2nd Degree AV Block Mobitz I

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** AV block  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Progressive prolongation of AV conduction until one atrial impulse fails to conduct.

**Criteria:**  
- PR interval progressively lengthens beat to beat
- Dropped QRS after the longest PR interval
- PR interval resets after the dropped beat
- Repeating pattern
- QRS often narrow

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Often benign and associated with increased vagal tone
- Can be seen at rest or during sleep
- Distinguish from blocked PACs

**Suggested Learning Sections:**  
- Recognition basics
- Diagnostic criteria
- Common pitfalls
- Differential diagnosis

**Common Misreads:**  
- Blocked PACs
- 1st degree AV block
- Mobitz II

**Related Topics:**  
- 2nd degree AV block Mobitz II
- Blocked PACs
- AV nodal block

**Comments for App Content:**  
- Teach progressive PR prolongation as the key feature

## 2nd Degree AV Block Mobitz II

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** AV block  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Intermittent failure of AV conduction without progressive PR prolongation.

**Criteria:**  
- PR interval constant before dropped beats
- Sudden dropped QRS complexes
- No progressive PR lengthening
- QRS may be wide
- Suggests infranodal conduction disease

**Clinical Notes:**  
- More serious than Mobitz I
- Higher risk of progression to complete heart block
- Often requires pacing evaluation

## 3rd Degree AV Block

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** AV block  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Complete failure of atrial impulses to conduct to the ventricles, producing AV dissociation.

**Criteria:**  
- P waves and QRS complexes have no fixed relationship
- Atrial and ventricular rhythms are independent
- Regular P-P intervals
- Regular R-R intervals
- Ventricular escape rhythm present
- Ventricular rate slower than atrial rate

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Medical emergency if symptomatic or unstable
- Escape rhythm may be junctional or ventricular
- Can present with syncope or profound bradycardia

**Suggested Learning Sections:**  
- Recognition basics
- Diagnostic criteria
- Clinical significance
- Differential diagnosis

**Common Misreads:**

- Junctional rhythm
- Sinus bradycardia

**Related Topics:**  
- Mobitz II

- Ventricular escape rhythm

**Comments for App Content:**  
- Teach AV dissociation clearly and visually if possible

## Idioventricular Rhythm

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Ventricular

- Regular rhythm
- Wide QRS complexes
- No normal P waves or AV dissociation

## Premature Ventricular Contractions

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Ventricular  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Early ventricular depolarizations originating below the AV node.

**Criteria:**  
- Premature wide QRS complex
- QRS usually ≥120 ms
- No preceding normal P wave
- Often followed by a compensatory pause
- T wave typically opposite in polarity to the QRS
- May be monomorphic or polymorphic

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Common and often benign when isolated
- Frequent PVCs may be associated with structural heart disease or electrolyte abnormality
- Can occur in patterns such as bigeminy or trigeminy

## Ventricular Tachycardia

**Type:** Rhythm  
**Category:** Ventricular  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Rapid ventricular rhythm originating in the ventricles, usually defined as three or more consecutive ventricular beats.

**Criteria:**  
- Wide QRS complexes
- Rate typically 100–250 bpm
- Regular or slightly irregular rhythm
- AV dissociation may be present
- Fusion beats or capture beats may be present
- Often monomorphic or polymorphic

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Potentially life-threatening
- Stable appearance does not exclude serious risk
- Wide-complex tachycardia should be treated as VT until proven otherwise in many training contexts

## Left Bundle Branch Block

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** Conduction  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Delay or block in left bundle conduction causing abnormal left ventricular depolarization.

**Criteria:**  
- QRS duration ≥120 ms
- Broad or notched R waves in lateral leads
- Deep S waves in right precordial leads
- Absent normal septal Q waves in lateral leads
- Secondary ST-T changes opposite the QRS direction

**Clinical Notes:**  
- Can obscure ischemic ST changes
- Often associated with structural heart disease
- Important conduction abnormality rather than a rhythm

## Left Anterior Fascicular Block

**Type:** Block  
**Category:** Conduction  
**Teaching Priority:** Medium  
**Definition:** Conduction delay in the left anterior fascicle causing left axis deviation.

- Left axis deviation
- qR pattern in aVL
- rS pattern in inferior leads
- QRS usually <120 ms or only mildly prolonged

## Anterior STEMI

**Type:** Pattern  
**Category:** Ischemic  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** Acute anterior ischemic injury pattern consistent with LAD territory occlusion.

**Criteria:**  
- ST elevation in contiguous anterior leads
- Reciprocal ST depression may be present in inferior leads
- Loss of R wave progression or evolving Q waves may be present
- May meet STEMI criteria in appropriate clinical context

## Left Ventricular Hypertrophy with Strain Pattern

**Type:** Pattern  
**Category:** Hypertrophy  
**Teaching Priority:** High  
**Definition:** ECG pattern of increased left ventricular voltage with secondary repolarization changes.

**Criteria:**  
- Increased QRS voltage meeting LVH criteria
- ST depression in lateral leads
- T-wave inversion in lateral leads
- Secondary repolarization changes opposite the major QRS direction
- Delayed intrinsicoid deflection may be present

**Clinical Notes:**
