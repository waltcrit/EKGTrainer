# AHA/ACC Rhythm and Diagnosis Definitions for EKGTrainer

This document provides AHA/ACC-based definitions and diagnostic criteria for all rhythm types in our cases database. Use this as the authoritative reference for clinical impressions and morphological findings.

---

## SINUS RHYTHMS

### Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)
**Definition:** Rhythm originating from the sinoatrial (SA) node with regular rate and normal conduction.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: 60–100 bpm
- Regular rhythm
- Upright P wave in lead II before each QRS
- PR interval: 120–200 ms
- QRS duration: <120 ms
- 1:1 P:QRS ratio

---

### Sinus Bradycardia
**Definition:** Regular sinus rhythm with rate < 60 bpm.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: < 60 bpm
- Regular rhythm
- Normal P wave morphology (upright in II)
- Normal PR interval (120–200 ms)
- Narrow QRS (<120 ms)
- All other features of NSR preserved

**Clinical Notes:** Common in athletes and during sleep. Treat only if symptomatic (hypotension, altered mental status, hemodynamic instability).

---

### Sinus Tachycardia
**Definition:** Regular sinus rhythm with rate > 100 bpm.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: > 100 bpm (often 100–160 bpm)
- Regular rhythm
- Upright P wave in lead II
- Normal PR interval (may appear short relative to RP interval at faster rates)
- Narrow QRS (<120 ms)
- 1:1 P:QRS ratio

**Clinical Notes:** Always identify underlying cause: pain, fever, hypovolemia, anxiety, infection, PE, thyrotoxicosis, or anemia. Is a response to increased sympathetic tone or decreased vagal tone.

---

### Sinus Arrhythmia

**Definition:** Sinus rhythm with physiologic variation in heart rate related to respiration (most common) or other cyclic variations.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Regular P waves (sinus origin, upright in II)
- Variation in PP intervals (and therefore RR intervals) > 120 ms
- Variation cyclically related to respiration
- Normal PR interval and QRS duration
- No ectopic beats

**Clinical Notes:** Normal finding, especially in young people and athletes. No treatment required.

---

## ATRIAL ARRHYTHMIAS

### Atrial Fibrillation (AF)
**Definition:** Rapid, irregular atrial arrhythmia characterized by uncoordinated atrial activation and loss of effective atrial mechanical function.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Replacement of P waves with rapid oscillations or fibrillatory waves
- Fibrillatory waves vary in amplitude, shape, and timing
- Irregular, rapid ventricular response (when AV conduction intact)
- RR intervals are irregularly irregular
- Baseline undulation without discrete P waves
- No isoelectric line between deflections

**Rate Classification:**
- AF with RVR (rapid ventricular response): Ventricular rate typically >100–120 bpm
- AF with controlled rate: Ventricular rate < 100 bpm

**Stages (2023 Guidelines):**
- Stage A: Risk factors present, no AF detected
- Stage B: P-wave abnormalities or other precursors
- Stage C: Documented AF
- Stage D: Structural or functional cardiac changes from AF

---

### Atrial Flutter (4:1)
**Definition:** Rapid, regular atrial arrhythmia usually arising from the right atrium with regular ventricular response due to fixed AV block.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Sawtooth or flutter waves (F waves) at regular rate typically 250–350 bpm
- Regular RR intervals
- Fixed AV block ratio (commonly 2:1, 3:1, or 4:1)
- QRS complexes narrow unless aberrant conduction
- Atrial rate and degree of AV block determine ventricular rate

**ECG Features:**
- F waves best seen in leads II, III, aVF (inferior leads) and V1
- Saw-tooth appearance

---

### Premature Atrial Contractions (PACs)
**Definition:** Ectopic atrial depolarization occurring earlier than expected sinus beat, originating outside the SA node.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- P wave morphology different from sinus P waves
- Occurs prematurely relative to sinus rhythm
- Usually followed by a QRS complex (unless blocked)
- Pause after PAC is non-compensatory (less than fully compensatory)
- PR interval may be different from sinus beats
- Can trigger SVT or AF if frequent

**Blocked PACs:** P wave occurs within or soon after preceding T wave; no QRS follows.

---

## SUPRAVENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA (SVT)

### SVT (Supraventricular Tachycardia)
**Definition:** Tachycardia originating above the ventricular level, most commonly due to AV nodal reentry (AVNRT, ~50%), AV reentry (AVRT, ~30%), or atrial tachycardia (~20%).

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Heart rate typically 140–250 bpm
- Regular narrow-complex tachycardia (QRS < 120 ms unless aberrant conduction)
- Abrupt onset and termination
- P waves may be buried in QRS or T wave or visible on baseline
- 1:1 AV conduction
- Adenosine-responsive (diagnostic and therapeutic)

---

## JUNCTIONAL RHYTHMS

### Junctional Rhythm
**Definition:** Abnormal rhythm originating from AV node or junction (40–60 bpm escape rate).

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: 40–60 bpm (junctional escape rhythm)
- Regular rhythm with constant intervals
- Narrow QRS complexes (<120 ms) — impulse travels via bundle of His
- P waves absent or inverted
- If P visible: retrograde conduction (P may appear before, during, or after QRS)
- AV dissociation possible

**Rate Classifications:**
- Junctional bradycardia: < 40 bpm
- Junctional escape rhythm: 40–60 bpm
- Accelerated junctional rhythm: 60–100 bpm
- Junctional tachycardia: > 100 bpm

---

### Accelerated Junctional Rhythm
**Definition:** Junctional rhythm with rate 60–100 bpm, faster than typical escape but slower than pathologic tachycardia.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: 60–100 bpm
- Regular rhythm
- Narrow QRS (<120 ms)
- P waves absent or inverted
- May represent enhanced automaticity of AV node
- Generally benign, often seen post-MI

---

### Idioventricular Rhythm
**Definition:** Automatic escape rhythm originating in the ventricular myocardium, usually due to severe bradycardia or AV block.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rate: 20–40 bpm (slower than ventricular escape)
- Regular rhythm
- Wide QRS complexes (≥120 ms, often 140–180 ms)
- Absence of P waves or AV dissociation
- Last-resort escape mechanism when junctional pacemakers fail
- Often seen in ischemia, hyperkalemia, or severe conduction block

---

## ATRIOVENTRICULAR (AV) BLOCKS

### 1st Degree AV Block
**Definition:** Delayed AV conduction with all atrial impulses reaching ventricles, but with prolongation of conduction time.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- PR interval > 200 ms (0.20 s) in adults
- All P waves followed by QRS complexes
- QRS, P wave, and baseline morphology normal
- 1:1 AV conduction maintained
- Regular rhythm
- May be normal variant or indicate structural/medication effect

---

### 2nd Degree AV Block — Mobitz I (Wenckebach)
**Definition:** Progressive prolongation of PR interval until one atrial impulse is not conducted, then resets.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- PR interval progressively lengthens beat-to-beat
- Dropped QRS after longest PR interval
- After dropped beat, PR interval resets to shorter interval
- Pattern repeats
- RR intervals become progressively shorter, then longest interval is after dropped beat
- Narrow QRS unless aberrant conduction
- Usually benign, often at rest or with increased vagal tone

---

### 2nd Degree AV Block — Mobitz II
**Definition:** Sudden, unpredictable loss of AV conduction without prior PR prolongation; indicates infranodal block.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- PR interval constant (no progressive prolongation)
- Periodic dropped QRS complexes
- Block occurs suddenly without warning
- QRS often wide (≥120 ms) due to infranodal location
- High risk of progression to 3rd degree block
- More serious than Mobitz I; may require pacing

**Distinction from Mobitz I:**
- Mobitz II: constant PR, sudden block
- Mobitz I: lengthening PR, then eventual block

---

### 3rd Degree (Complete) AV Block
**Definition:** Complete loss of AV conduction; no atrial impulses reach the ventricles. Atrial and ventricular rhythms are independent.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Regular P waves at one rate (atrial rate, typically 60–100 bpm)
- Regular QRS complexes at slower rate (ventricular escape rate)
- No fixed relationship between P waves and QRS complexes (AV dissociation)
- P-QRS dissociation: P waves march through QRS and T waves
- Ventricular rate determined by escape pacemaker location:
  - **Junctional escape:** 40–60 bpm, narrow QRS
  - **Ventricular escape:** 20–40 bpm, wide QRS
- Atrial rate > ventricular rate
- **Key finding:** Atrial rate significantly faster than ventricular rate (>1.3× difference) with regular P-P and regular R-R intervals

---

## PREMATURE BEATS AND ECTOPY

### Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)
**Definition:** Ectopic ventricular depolarizations occurring prematurely, bypassing normal conduction system.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Wide QRS complexes (≥120 ms)
- Premature relative to expected sinus beat
- Often with compensatory pause (full pause = two sinus intervals)
- T wave opposite in polarity to QRS (wide secondary T wave)
- No P wave preceding PVC, or retrograde P wave after QRS
- Morphology may be monomorphic (same) or polymorphic (varying)

**Patterns:**
- **Bigeminy:** Every other beat is a PVC
- **Trigeminy:** Every third beat is a PVC
- **Couplets/runs:** 2–3 or more consecutive PVCs

---

### Ventricular Bigeminy
**Definition:** Pattern of alternating sinus beat and PVC.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Repeating pattern: sinus beat, PVC, sinus beat, PVC
- Regular overall rate (but composed of two rhythms)
- Wide QRS for ectopic beat
- Usually uniform PVC morphology (monomorphic)

---

### Ventricular Trigeminy
**Definition:** Pattern of two sinus beats followed by a PVC, repeating regularly.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Pattern: sinus, sinus, PVC, sinus, sinus, PVC
- Regular overall sequence
- Usually monomorphic PVC morphology

---

## VENTRICULAR TACHYARRHYTHMIAS

### Ventricular Tachycardia (VTach)
**Definition:** Rapid ventricular arrhythmia originating from ventricular myocardium, usually ≥3 consecutive PVCs.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria (2017 Guideline):**
- Heart rate typically 100–250 bpm
- Wide QRS complexes (≥120 ms)
- Regular or slightly irregular rhythm
- **AV Dissociation:** P waves visible at different rate from QRS (pathognomonic for VT)
- **Fusion beats:** Narrow QRS where atrial and ventricular impulses collide
- **Capture beats:** Narrow QRS from sinus conduction interrupting VT (confirms VT diagnosis)
- QRS ≥140 ms supports VT diagnosis
- **Brugada Criteria** for VT vs. SVT with aberrancy:
  - Onset-to-nadir (R-peak) time ≥100 ms
  - Monophasic R/RR morphology
  - RS interval >100 ms in any precordial lead
  - No RS complex in any precordial lead
  - Concordance (all positive or all negative) in precordial leads

**Clinical Classification:**
- **Sustained VT:** >30 seconds duration, causing hemodynamic compromise
- **Non-sustained VT (NSVT):** < 30 seconds, often self-terminating

---

### Ventricular Fibrillation (VFib)
**Definition:** Chaotic, disorganized ventricular electrical activity resulting in no organized contraction and absence of perfusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Rapid, chaotic baseline oscillations
- No organized QRS complexes
- No identifiable P waves
- Amplitude varies (coarse vs. fine VFib)
- Incompatible with life; requires immediate defibrillation
- Organized by frequency bands: coarse (>200 microvolts) vs. fine (<200 microvolts)

---

### Asystole
**Definition:** Complete absence of electrical and mechanical cardiac activity.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- Flat baseline
- No P waves, QRS, or T waves
- May have occasional artifact from chest compressions or electrode movement
- Most severe form of cardiac arrest
- Poor prognosis

---

## BUNDLE BRANCH BLOCKS AND CONDUCTION DISTURBANCES

### Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)
**Definition:** Conduction delay or block in the left bundle, delaying depolarization of the left ventricle.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria (AHA/ACCF/HRS Standards 2009):**
- **QRS duration ≥120 ms**
- Broad, notched R wave (or RR') in lateral leads (I, aVL, V5–V6)
- Deep S wave in right precordial leads (V1–V3)
- Absent Q wave in lateral leads (I, aVL, V5–V6)
- **ST-segment and T-wave changes opposite to QRS direction** (secondary changes)
- Delayed intrinsicoid deflection in V5–V6 (>60 ms from QRS onset to peak)

**Clinical Significance:**
- Morphological diagnosis, not a rhythm
- Indicates significant conduction delay
- Common in hypertension, cardiomyopathy, aortic stenosis
- May mask STEMI (requires modified criteria)

---

### Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB)
**Definition:** Conduction delay or block in the right bundle, delaying depolarization of the right ventricle.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **QRS duration ≥120 ms**
- **RSR' pattern (M-shaped) in V1–V2** (rabbit ears)
- Wide S wave in lateral leads (I, V5–V6)
- T wave inversion in V1–V2 (secondary change)

**Clinical Significance:**
- Common in PE, chronic lung disease, atrial septal defect
- Can be chronic or acute
- May obscure STEMI diagnosis

---

### Left Anterior Fascicular Block (LAFB)
**Definition:** Conduction block in the anterior branch of the left bundle, altering QRS axis and morphology.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Marked left axis deviation (LAD):** QRS axis between –45° to –90° (or more negative)
- **Small r wave in lead III, with S wave > r wave** (rS pattern)
- **Small q wave and tall R wave in lead aVL** (qR pattern)
- QRS duration 100–120 ms (narrower than complete LBBB)
- Delayed intrinsicoid deflection in aVL

**Clinical Notes:**
- Most common fascicular block
- Often associated with inferior MI
- May be normal variant in thin-chested individuals

---

## ST-ELEVATION AND ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROMES

### Anterior STEMI
**Definition:** ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction of the anterior wall due to LAD (left anterior descending) coronary artery occlusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **ST elevation ≥1 mm in two contiguous precordial leads** (V1–V4)
- Loss of R wave progression or r/R ratio <1/3 in V2–V3
- Reciprocal ST depression in II, III, aVF
- Elevated cardiac biomarkers (troponin, CK-MB)
- Typical chest pain history

**Associated Features:**
- May have Q waves (acute or evolving)
- T-wave inversions develop after ST elevation phase
- May have right ventricular involvement (from proximal RV branch occlusion)

---

### Inferior STEMI
**Definition:** ST-segment elevation MI of the inferior wall due to RCA (right coronary artery) or LCx (left circumflex) occlusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **ST elevation ≥1 mm in two contiguous inferior leads** (II, III, aVF)
- Reciprocal ST depression in I, aVL, V1–V2
- May develop Q waves
- Elevated cardiac biomarkers

**Associated Features:**
- May have right ventricular involvement (RV MI)
- Often associated with bradycardia (vagal) or heart block
- May evolve with posterior extension

---

### Lateral STEMI
**Definition:** ST-segment elevation MI of the lateral wall due to LCx occlusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **ST elevation ≥1 mm in lateral leads** (I, aVL, V5–V6)
- May have reciprocal changes in inferior or precordial leads
- Elevated cardiac biomarkers
- Q waves may develop

---

### Posterior STEMI
**Definition:** ST-segment elevation MI of the posterior wall, often from RCA occlusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **ST depression in V1–V2** (mirror image of elevation)
- Tall R wave in V1 (mirror of Q wave)
- ST elevation in posterior leads (III, V8–V9 if available, or right lateral leads)
- May have inferior involvement
- Elevated biomarkers

---

### NSTEMI — ST Depression
**Definition:** Myocardial infarction without ST elevation, with ischemic ST changes and elevated biomarkers.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **ST depression ≥1 mm in two contiguous leads**
- Elevated troponin or CK-MB
- Clinical presentation with chest pain/anginal symptoms
- Likely subendocardial infarction
- T-wave inversion may be present
- No STEMI criteria met

---

### Wellens Syndrome — Type A and Type B
**Definition:** Reperfusion pattern after partial LAD occlusion/reperfusion, indicating critical LAD stenosis; STEMI equivalent.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Deeply inverted or biphasic T waves in V2–V3** (may extend to V4–V5)
- **Type A:** Biphasic T waves (positive then negative terminal portion)
- **Type B:** Deep, symmetric, inverted T waves
- ST segment usually <1 mm elevation or normal
- History of recent chest pain with reperfusion
- No loss of R wave (differentiates from acute MI with Q wave)
- Specific to LAD ischemia/reperfusion

**Clinical Significance:**
- High-risk pattern; indicates critical LAD stenosis
- Risk of reinfarction if not revascularized
- Requires urgent angiography

---

## PERICARDITIS AND INFLAMMATORY PATTERNS

### Pericarditis
**Definition:** Inflammation of the pericardium, manifesting with characteristic ECG evolution.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Stage 1:** PR depression (except in aVR, V1 where PR elevation) with diffuse ST elevation (≥0.5 mm)
- **Stage 2:** ST-segment normalization, may have PR depression
- **Stage 3:** Diffuse T-wave inversion (except aVR, V1)
- **Stage 4:** Return to normal baseline (may take weeks to months)
- Elevated CRP or ESR
- Pericardial friction rub
- Chest pain worse with inspiration and lying down
- Pericardial effusion on echo

---

## PULMONARY EMBOLISM PATTERNS

### Pulmonary Embolism — RV Strain Pattern
**Definition:** Right ventricular strain from acute pulmonary hypertension due to PE.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **T-wave inversion in leads V1–V4** (right precordial)
- May have ST depression in V1–V2
- Sinus tachycardia (if hemodynamically significant)
- May have atrial fibrillation
- RR' pattern or RBBB morphology
- Normal QRS duration unless RBBB present
- Classic pattern: tall R/S ratio in aVR

---

### Pulmonary Embolism — S1Q3T3
**Definition:** Classic but uncommon PE pattern showing acute right heart strain.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **S wave in lead I** (deep negative deflection)
- **Q wave in lead III**
- **T-wave inversion in lead III**
- Pattern indicates acute RV overload with dilation and R-axis shift
- May also show T-wave inversion in V1–V2
- Not present in majority of PEs but highly specific when found

---

## VENTRICULAR HYPERTROPHY AND STRAIN PATTERNS

### Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) with Strain Pattern
**Definition:** Ventricular remodeling with increased wall thickness causing ischemic changes.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Increased QRS voltage:** 
  - Sokolow-Lyon criterion: SV1 + RV5/V6 ≥35 mm
  - Cornell criterion: RaVL + SV3 ≥20 mm (women) or ≥28 mm (men)
- **Left axis deviation** (optional)
- **ST-segment depression in lateral leads** (I, aVL, V5–V6)
- **Symmetric T-wave inversion in lateral leads** (strain pattern)
- **Delayed intrinsicoid deflection** in V5–V6 (>60 ms)
- Commonly seen in hypertension

**Clinical Notes:**
- Indicates chronic hypertension or aortic stenosis
- Strain pattern indicates pressure overload physiology
- Associated with increased cardiovascular risk

---

### RV Strain Pattern
**Definition:** Right ventricular pressure overload causing ischemic T-wave changes.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **T-wave inversion in V1–V4** (right precordial)
- Vertical QRS axis (right axis deviation)
- May have S wave in lead I, Q wave in lead III
- Elevated P waves (P pulmonale)
- Seen in PE, pulmonary hypertension, chronic lung disease, acute RV infarction

---

## LEFT AND RIGHT ATRIAL ENLARGEMENT

### Left Atrial Enlargement (LAE)
**Definition:** Increased atrial mass from prolonged volume or pressure overload.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Broad, bifid (notched) P wave in lead II** (duration ≥120 ms)
- **Deep, wide negative deflection in the terminal portion of P in V1**
- P wave axis may shift leftward
- Often associated with prolonged PR interval
- Seen in hypertension, mitral valve disease, dilated cardiomyopathy

---

### Right Atrial Enlargement (RAE)
**Definition:** Increased right atrial mass from chronic lung disease or RV overload.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Tall, peaked P waves in inferior leads (II, III, aVF)** (P pulmonale; >2.5 mm)
- **Tall P in aVR** (right atrial lead)
- Right axis deviation (QRS axis >90°)
- May have right axis shift
- Seen in COPD, pulmonary hypertension, chronic RV overload

---

## CHANNELOPATHIES

### Long QT Syndrome (LQTS)
**Definition:** Genetic or acquired prolongation of repolarization increasing risk of Torsades de Pointes and sudden cardiac death.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **QTc ≥500 ms** — diagnostic in absence of secondary causes
- **QTc 480–499 ms** with unexplained syncope and no secondary cause
- Syncope with exertion or auditory triggers (type-specific)
- Family history of sudden death
- Risk of Torsades de Pointes (polymorphic VT with changing axis)
- Multiple genetic subtypes (LQT1, LQT2, LQT3, etc.)

**QTc Calculation:**
- QTc = QT / √(RR in seconds)
- Bazett formula widely used; Fridericia sometimes preferred

**Clinical Notes:**
- Beta-blockers first-line for LQT1 and LQT2
- Avoid QT-prolonging medications
- High-risk arrhythmogenic condition

---

### Brugada Syndrome — Type 1
**Definition:** Genetic sodium channelopathy predisposing to sudden cardiac death, especially during sleep.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Type 1 ECG Pattern (diagnostic):**
  - **Coved ST-segment elevation ≥2 mm in V1–V2**
  - **Inverted T wave immediately after ST elevation** (coved appearance)
  - **J point elevation** (junction of QRS and ST segment)
- **Type 2 and Type 3:** Saddleback patterns with 1–2 mm elevation (less specific)
- Type 1 pattern may be unmasked by fever, tricyclic antidepressants, or sodium channel blockers (ajmaline, procainamide, flecainide)
- Risk of Brugada-associated VF

**Clinical Notes:**
- More common in Southeast Asian populations
- High sudden nocturnal unexplained nocturnal death syndrome (SUNDS) in endemic areas
- Electrolyte abnormalities (especially hypokalemia) increase risk
- ICD indicated for symptomatic patients

## PACEMAKER RHYTHMS

### Atrial Pacing (AAI)
**Definition:** Pacemaker delivers stimuli only to the atrium; ventricle conducts normally.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Pacing spike before each P wave** (atrial spike)
- Normal QRS complexes following each P wave
- 1:1 AV conduction maintained
- No ventricular pacing spikes
- PR interval conducted normally
- Used for bradycardia without AV block

---

### AV Sequential Pacing (DDD)
**Definition:** Dual-chamber pacemaker sensing and pacing both atria and ventricles with appropriate AV delay.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Atrial pacing spike followed by P wave**
- **Appropriate AV delay** (typically 100–200 ms, shorter than sinus PR)
- **Ventricular pacing spike followed by QRS complex** at end of AV delay
- Both chambers show 100% capture
- Normal QRS morphology unless underlying conduction abnormality
- Most physiologic pacing mode (maintains AV synchrony)

---

### Ventricular Pacing (VVI)
**Definition:** Ventricular demand pacemaker; delivers stimulus only when intrinsic ventricular rate falls below threshold.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Pacing spike before each QRS complex**
- **Normally conducts sensed beats without spike** (demand mode)
- Wide QRS complexes (≥120 ms) — paced beats
- Loss of AV synchrony (pacing spike has no relation to P waves)
- No atrial pacing visible
- Used for bradycardia when AV block present or when maintaining AV synchrony not possible

**Normal Findings:**
- 100% ventricular capture
- Appropriate sensing (no spikes on intrinsic beats above threshold)
- Stable pacing threshold

---

## ELECTRICAL ABNORMALITIES AND OTHER PATTERNS

### Electrical Alternans
**Definition:** Alternating QRS-complex (or T-wave) amplitude on successive beats, suggesting mechanical instability or pericardial effusion.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Alternating tall and short QRS complexes** beat-to-beat
- Regular rhythm
- May involve P waves, T waves, or entire complex
- Often indicates large pericardial effusion with cardiac swinging
- High mortality risk (hemodynamic compromise)
- Associated with tamponade physiology

---

### Hyperkalemia
**Definition:** Elevated serum potassium causing characteristic ECG changes.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Peaked, narrow T waves** (tent-shaped) in precordial leads
- **Widened QRS** (>120 ms)
- **Prolonged PR interval**
- **Flattened P waves** (may disappear at severe levels)
- **Sine-wave pattern** at very high levels (severe hyperkalemia)
- ST depression
- Severity correlates with K+ level and rate of rise

**Clinical Notes:**
- ECG changes appear at K+ >6.0–6.5 mEq/L
- Requires urgent treatment to avoid VF
- Calcium gluconate stabilizes membrane; insulin + glucose/beta-agonists shift K+ intracellularly

---

### Pathological Q Waves (Old MI Pattern)
**Definition:** Scarred myocardium from prior myocardial infarction, indicated by persistent Q waves without acute ST changes.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Q waves ≥0.04 seconds (1 small square) in width**
- **Q wave depth ≥25% of R-wave height** in the same lead
- Specific territory: anterior (V1–V4), inferior (II, III, aVF), or lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6)
- Normal ST-segment and T-wave (unless acute change)
- Indicates fibrotic scar tissue

**Clinical Notes:**
- Does not indicate acute ischemia
- Indicates previous transmural infarction
- Important for risk stratification

---

### WPW (Wolff-Parkinson-White) Syndrome
**Definition:** Accessory pathway (bypass tract) conducting atrial impulses directly to ventricles, bypassing AV node, predisposing to reentrant SVT.

**AHA/ACC Diagnostic Criteria:**
- **Short PR interval** (<120 ms) — direct accessory pathway conduction shorter than AV node
- **Delta wave** — initial slurred portion of QRS from accessory pathway depolarization starting
- **Wide QRS** (≥120 ms) due to fusion of accessory pathway and AV node conduction
- Accessory pathway location determines QRS axis and morphology
- Prone to orthodromic (narrow-complex) and antidromic (wide-complex) reentrant tachycardias
